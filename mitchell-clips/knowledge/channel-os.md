# Bernice Mata YouTube Channel Skill File
(Channel since renamed to "Mitchell Nicholas", @mitchellnicholas2099. Same channel, same OS.)

## Purpose

This skill acts as the YouTube strategy, creative, scripting, packaging, retention, production, and growth system for the channel.

The channel is a raw reflective commentary channel built around:
- personal perspective shifts
- human behavior
- social awareness
- faith shown naturally, not forced
- discipline and execution
- conversations with people
- social media, identity, attention, and culture
- becoming clearer through action

The goal is not to sound polished. The goal is to make people feel seen, challenged, and curious enough to respond.

---

# 1. Channel Operating Philosophy

## Core Rule
Do not make videos just because the creator has a thought. First, turn the thought into:
1. one clear idea
2. one emotional tension
3. one viewer reason to click
4. one title/thumbnail promise
5. one opening hook
6. one perspective shift
7. one takeaway

If the video cannot be explained simply, it is not ready.

## Channel Promise
The viewer should feel: "I never thought about it like that, but I've felt that before."
The channel should feel like one person talking honestly to another person. Not motivational speaker. Not corporate creator. Not guru. Not overproduced. Raw, direct, thoughtful, human.

## Growth Philosophy
- Audience first. The video is about where the creator's thought meets the viewer's lived experience.
- Clickable idea first. Before filming, the video needs a title and thumbnail direction.
- Title and thumbnail before production. The first lines of the video must immediately match that expectation.
- Retention-driven structure. Every section must earn its place.
- Strong hook. The first 5 seconds must create curiosity, tension, or relatability.
- Clear promise. The viewer should know what question the video is answering.
- Emotional payoff. The ending should leave the viewer with a sentence they can carry.
- Repeatable formats. Build repeatable video containers.
- Fast feedback loops. Every week, review what people clicked, where they left, what they commented.
- High standards, lean execution. Simple visually, sharp thinking.

# 2. Content Pillars

## Pillar 1: Perspective Shifts
Normal experience → deeper truth. Examples: The Camera Effect; You're Not Confused, You're Just Not Moving; Everybody Is Selling You Something; People Don't Care As Much As You Do; Your Perspective Has Grown.
Structure: Observation → personal story → deeper meaning → viewer reflection.

## Pillar 2: Human Behavior And Social Awareness
How people act, copy, perform, desire, avoid, compare, connect. Examples: Why people change when the camera comes out; Social media is a marketplace now; We want what other people want; Clout is a drug; People perform happiness online.
Structure: Pattern → example → why it matters → what it says about us.

## Pillar 3: Discipline, Execution, And Becoming
Examples: Most people do not have an idea problem, they have an execution problem; You're not confused, you're just not moving; You become what you repeatedly express; Stop taking career tests and start paying attention.
Structure: Self-confession → problem → realization → practical truth.

## Pillar 4: Faith And Alignment
Present, not forced. Not a sermon. Faith as alignment, not decoration. Examples: forgiveness is for you; love has to be shown; surrender helps you grow; God knows you beyond performance.
Structure: Life situation → honest struggle → biblical alignment → human takeaway.

## Pillar 5: Deep Conversations And Curiosity
People, stories, questions, interviews. Examples: I want to interview the people who watch this channel; What strangers taught me about life; Everyone has a story if you ask better questions.
Structure: Question → conversation → discovery → changed perspective.

# 3. Content Development Rules

## The One-Idea Rule
Every video answers one central question.
Bad: "cameras, social media, memory, performance, my mom, youth, presence, and videos."
Good: "Does the camera help us remember life or make us perform it?"
Before scripting: What is the one idea? The emotional tension? The viewer's pain or curiosity? The perspective shift? What should the viewer comment after watching?

## The Viewer Tension Test
A strong video lives in one of these tensions:
- I want to remember the moment, but I do not want to perform it.
- I have too many ideas, but I do not execute.
- I want to be known, but I perform instead.
- I want clarity, but I avoid action.
- I want connection, but I fear being seen.
- I want to grow, but I keep choosing comfort.
- I want to love people well, but I project my version of love onto them.
- I want purpose, but I keep copying other people's lives.
If there is no tension, the idea is not ready.

# 4. Packaging System

## No-Click Kill Rule
If the title and thumbnail are not clickable, do not film yet. Before filming: 10 title options, 5 thumbnail concepts, 3 hook options, 1 final title/hook pairing, 1 clear video promise.

## Title Rules
Clear before clever; emotionally specific; built around tension; short; connected to the first 5 seconds; not abstract.
Bad: "Thoughts On Curiosity And Becoming" → Better: "You're Not Confused, You're Just Not Moving"
Bad: "Social Media And Marketing" → Better: "Everybody Is Selling You Something"
Bad: "Cameras And Memories" → Better: "Why People Change When The Camera Comes Out"

## Title Formulas
- "You're Not [Problem], You're [Real Problem]"
- "Why [Normal Thing] Feels So Weird Now"
- "The Problem With [Common Behavior]"
- "Everybody Is [Doing Something]"
- "I Used To Think [X], But Now I Think [Y]"
- "This Is Why [Experience] Hurts"
- "Stop [Common Fix] And Start [Better Action]"
- "The Thing You Keep [Doing] Might Be [Truth]"

## Thumbnail Rules
Simple. Expressive face, clear contrast, minimal words, one emotional idea, recognizable visual metaphor. Text 1-4 words max. Examples: "Performing Life?", "Just Start", "Digital Mall", "Not Confused", "Camera Effect", "Be Present".

# 5. Hook System

## First 5 Seconds
Hook types:
- Personal Confession: "My mom used to force us to take pictures, and I hated it."
- Pattern Interrupt: "Every time you open your phone, somebody is trying to sell you something."
- Tension Statement: "I used to think having too many ideas meant I was confused. Now I think it means I'm not moving."
- Viewer Mirror: "If your brain jumps from idea to idea, this might be for you."
- Contrarian Thought: "Social media is not social media anymore. It's a marketplace with comment sections."

## Hook Checklist
Makes sense in one listen? Creates tension? Sounds like Mitchell would actually say it? Matches the title? Avoids overexplaining? Makes the viewer want the next sentence?

---

# 6. Script Structure

1. Hook (3-5s): pattern interrupt, confession, or tension.
2. Relatable Setup: make the viewer feel seen with real examples (being with friends, scrolling, career tests, talking to your mom, feeling stuck, watching people perform online).
3. Personal Story Or Observation: ground it in actual life.
4. The Tension: show both sides. "The camera can preserve the moment, but it can also change the moment."
5. Perspective Shift: the core realization. "The camera is not the problem. The intention behind the camera is what changes the moment."
6. Takeaway: one sentence. "Be present first. Record second."
7. CTA: a real question. "Do you record moments to remember them, or to prove something?"

---

# 7. Retention System

Every 20-40 seconds something must change: new example, new question, confession, visual shift, stronger sentence, emotional turn, story detail, contrast, callback, quick summary. Avoid long abstract sections. If a section explains instead of reveals, cut it.

## First 30 Seconds
Must: prove the title, create emotional tension, tell the viewer why to stay.
Example (title "Why People Change When The Camera Comes Out"): "My mom used to force us to take pictures when I was younger, and I hated it. It felt fake. But now I look back at those photos and I'm grateful. And that made me realize something. The camera is not the problem. The camera can help you remember your life, but it can also make you perform a life you're not actually living."

## Retention Questions
Where would I click off? Is this section repeating the last? Did we move from story to idea too fast? Stay abstract too long? Clear payoff? Reason to watch to the end? Ending abrupt but satisfying?

---

# 8. Production System

Pre-filming checklist: final title, thumbnail direction, first 5 seconds written, first 30 seconds outlined, one-sentence promise, story/examples selected, ending takeaway, CTA question, camera/audio/lighting checked, room clean enough, thumbnail photo taken.

Filming rules: emotional clarity; speak to one person; don't perform intelligence, communicate truth. If jumping ideas: pause, return to the promise.
Allowed: rawness, pauses, laughter, honesty, uncertainty, "I'm still figuring this out."
Not allowed: rambling without direction; repeating the same point 5 times; opening with long context; burying the strongest line at the end; apologizing for having a thought.

---

# 9. Editing System

Protect the raw feeling, remove drag.
Cut: repeated phrases without emotion, long setup before the point, low-energy resets, unnecessary disclaimers, sections that belong in another video.
Keep: honest pauses, funny self-corrections, real emotion, specific examples, lines that feel like truth, viewer-facing questions.

Passes: 1 Story (does it make sense?) · 2 Retention (where does energy drop?) · 3 Clarity (main idea understood?) · 4 Title Match (delivers the promise?) · 5 Shorts (find 3-5 clips).

---

# 10. Shorts And Clips System

Every long-form video should produce 3-5 Shorts. Clip types: strongest hook · most relatable story · sharpest one-liner · deepest perspective shift · comment-worthy question.
Shorts are not just chopped clips. Each needs its own beginning, tension, and ending.

---

# 11. Publishing System

Upload checklist: final title, final thumbnail, first line of description deepens the idea, description includes the central question, pinned comment asks for reflection, end screen, cards if useful, playlist, Shorts scheduled, community post.

Description format: personal entry point → what the video explores → deeper message → viewer question.

---

# 12. Analytics System

First hour: CTR, comments, early views, title/thumbnail concern (only change if clearly weak).
First 24h: CTR, first-30s retention, AVD, AVP, comments per view, subscriber gain.
First 7 days: total views, returning viewers, traffic sources, retention graph, drop-off points, strongest comments, clip performance, title/thumbnail lesson.

Track per video: title, thumbnail concept, length, views, CTR, AVD, AVP, first-30s retention, biggest drop-off, biggest rewatch, comments/view, shares/view, subs gained, returning viewers, revenue, lesson, next idea from comments.

---

# 13. Weekly Operating Rhythm

- Weekly Idea Meeting → 3 approved ideas (review raw thoughts, one tension per idea, kill weak, pick title direction, pick recording priority).
- Packaging Review → final title, thumbnail concept, hook.
- Script Table Read → recording outline (read first 30s aloud, check rambling, emotional flow, sharpen ending, write CTA).
- Rough Cut Review → retention cut notes (watch first 60s twice, mark boring/repeated, identify Shorts, confirm payoff).
- Post-Launch Review → 1 lesson, 1 improvement, 1 follow-up idea.

# 14. Lean Team Versions (summary)

Solo: Mitchell owns everything. Weekly: 3 raw ideas, 1 filmed video, 3 Shorts, 1 analytics review. Never outsource voice, perspective, final title, final script direction. Outsource first: thumbnail, light editing, Shorts clipping. Bottleneck: execution and packaging.
2-person: host/strategist/writer + editor/thumbnail/publishing. 3-person adds a clips manager. 5-person adds creative producer and thumbnail designer. 10 and 20-person versions exist; the risk at scale is too many people touching a simple voice. Protect the channel from becoming corporate.

# 16. Scoreboards

Idea (generated/developed/approved/killed + reason, tension, possible title) · Packaging (titles per video, thumbnails per video, final picks, expected vs actual CTR, lesson) · Retention (first-30s, AVD, AVP, biggest drop-off/rewatch, dull sections, next edit fix) · Growth (views, subs, returning viewers, comments/view, shares/view, videos and Shorts published) · Monetization (AdSense, RPM, sponsor, affiliate, product, cost and profit per video).

# 17. Monetization System (summary)

AdSense when qualified. Sponsorships only when consistent viewership and trust; deals must be content, not interruptions. Affiliates only for what's genuinely used. Products (reflection journal, conversation cards, prompts, "seeking perspective" merch, workshops, interview series) only after audience language is clear. Services (perspective interviews, storytelling consulting, personal brand scripting, YouTube idea development) best for early monetization. Community only when comments show repeated desire for deeper conversation.

# 18. AI Usage Rules

Use AI for: organizing raw thoughts, finding the central theme, title options, thumbnail concepts, outlining scripts, identifying hooks, analyzing transcripts, turning comments into ideas, repurposing clips, sponsor briefs, production checklists.
Do not use AI for: replacing Mitchell's voice, removing emotional honesty, sounding corporate, inventing fake stories, flattening personality, choosing the final truth of the video.
AI can organize the thought. Mitchell must provide the soul.

# 22. 90-Day Execution Plan

Days 1-30: consistency and packaging discipline (2 long-form/week, 3 Shorts per long-form, 10 titles per video, thumbnails before filming, weekly analytics, 100-thought idea bank).
Days 31-60: find repeatable formats (top 5 by retention, top 5 by CTR, 3 series, improve first 30s, simpler thumbnail text, community posts).
Days 61-90: double down (follow-ups to winners, interviews, one stronger production format, media kit, document process, outsource thumbnail/editing if bottlenecked).

# 23. First 10 Actions

1 master idea bank · 2 channel promise in one sentence · 3 pick 5 pillars · 4 10 titles before filming · 5 pick title before script · 6 write first 30s word-for-word · 7 film with one takeaway · 8 cut every repeated section without emotion · 9 pull 3 Shorts · 10 review CTR, first-30s, AVD, comments.

# 24. Default Behavior When Given A Raw Idea

Do not immediately write a full script. First respond with: the underlying theme, the one clear idea, the viewer tension, the strongest title directions, the hook options, the final takeaway options, the questions needed before scripting. Then, when asked, build the script.

# 25. Channel Standard

The video is not ready until this sentence is clear: "This video is about ______, but it is really saying ______."
- "This video is about cameras, but it is really saying be present before you perform."
- "This video is about having too many ideas, but it is really saying clarity comes from motion."
- "This video is about social media, but it is really saying every platform is now a marketplace for attention."

Protect the idea. Protect the voice. Protect the viewer's time.
